SER // ESTAR
ser = what it is  ·  estar = how it stands  ·  every tense, both verbs

// dim = stem  ·  bright = ending  ·  tap a tense for its page

SER names what something is.  ESTAR says how or where it stands right now.

"Permanent vs temporary" is the trap version of this rule. A dead man está muerto forever, and tonight's wedding es in Lantana for three hours. Ask what kind of thing you are saying, and ignore how long it lasts.

// decide in order
  1. Is it where an event happens, or when it happens?La boda es en Lantana. La cena es a las siete.
    SER
  2. Is it where a person or thing is?Las copas están en el carrito.
    ESTAR
  3. Is a noun coming next (a job, a name, a kind of thing)?Soy capitán. Fue un éxito.
    SER
  4. Is it a word made from a verb, ending in -ado / -ido (or muerto, roto)? Frozen flux.El salón está cerrado. La copa está rota.
    ESTAR
  5. Is it -ing, an action in progress?Estamos sirviendo la ensalada.
    ESTAR
  6. Adjective left over: is it the thing's nature, or its condition at the moment?El chef es creativo. / El café está frío.
    SER / ESTAR

Ser is three Latin verbs stitched into one. Estar comes from one.

AncestorMeaningForms it gaveIts job
esseto existsoy, eres, es, somos, son · era, eras, éramosThe present and the background past: something existing in a state.
fu-to grow, becomefui, fue, fueron · fuera, fueseThe finished past: what something turned out to be. Shared with ir, so fui = I was and I went.
sedereto sitser, sea, siendo, sido · seré, sería · séThe verb's name, the subjunctive, -ing and -ed, and everything built on the name (future, conditional).
stareto standestar, estoy, estuve, estaba, estéAll of estar. Standing in a place, a posture, a condition.

sedere → ser

the infinitive comes from "to sit"
sedere→seer→ser

The d between vowels wore away, and so did the final e. Old Spanish wrote seer, and the two e's merged into ser. The same root gives la Santa Sede, the Holy See, the seat.

stare → estar

"to stand," with an e added in front
estare=estar

Spanish can't start a word with s + consonant, so it adds an e: schola → escuela, Hispania → España, studium → estudio. Stabat Mater, "the Mother was standing," uses the same verb.

// why some tenses look the way they do
Future
The infinitive plus haber: ser + he → seré. Medieval Spanish said "ser he," literally "I have to be," and the two words fused.
Conditional
The infinitive plus the old past of haber: ser + hía → sería. Same fusion, one step back in time.
Imperf. subj.
Built from the preterite. Take the ellos form (fueron, estuvieron), drop -ron, add -ra or -se. That's why it shares the fu- root.
Compound
Haber + the participle (sido / estado). Only haber changes. The participle stays the same in every person.
TRAP

"Permanent = ser, temporary = estar" fails

Está muerto is permanent and takes estar. La boda es en Lantana lasts one night and takes ser. Use the decide-in-order list instead.

ACCENT

esta ≠ está

esta = this  ·  está = is. One mark, two different words. Always write it.

ACCENT

Every conditional form keeps the í

sería, serías, seríamos, serían · estaría, estaríamos. Without it, the stress falls on the wrong syllable.

ACCENT

Irregular preterites have no accent

fui, fue, estuve, estuvo are written plain. Regular ones keep it: llegó, preparó, sirvió.

ACCENT

Nosotros carries the accent in two tenses

Imperfect: éramos, estábamos. Imperfect subjunctive: fuéramos, estuviéramos. Every other person in those tenses is written plain.

FORM

Tú preterite never ends in -s

fuiste · fuistes    estuviste · estuvistes

FORM

Fuimos, never fuemos

The u holds through the whole tense: fui, fuiste, fue, fuimos, fueron.

FORM

Estuvieron, never estuvon

The ellos preterite of estar ends in -ieron. Ser's is fueron, since the i drops after fu-.

MEANING

Fui = I was AND I went

Ser and ir share the whole preterite. Fui al salón = I went to the ballroom. Fui capitán = I was a captain. Context decides.

MEANING

Fuera = "were" AND "outside"

Si fuera el gerente = If I were the manager. Está fuera = He's outside.

MEANING

Fue vs era

Fue gives the result once it's over (fue un éxito). Era paints the background (era tarde, eran las seis).

NOTE

Tú command of estar sounds odd alone

Speakers say estate (with te): estate quieto, stay still. For a team, use estén listos, be ready.
